## 2. THE 90-SECOND PITCH (use this in a meeting or call)

### Problem

> "Let me ask you something — when your reps open Salesforce, do they trust what they see? Most companies I work with say no. Contacts have missing emails, wrong titles, duplicated accounts, and leads that haven't been touched in two years sitting next to fresh ones. That means your outbound bounces, your forecasts are off, and any AI or scoring tools you bought are working off garbage."

### What I Do

> "I specialize in taking that messy CRM data and turning it into something your team can actually sell from. Here's what that looks like:
>
> **First**, I audit your data — I run it through my tools and show you exactly what's broken: how many duplicates, what percentage of contacts are missing key fields, how many are stale. I give you a scored health report.
>
> **Then**, I clean and enrich — I deduplicate records, standardize titles and industries, fill in missing emails and direct dials using AI and data providers. Every record becomes a full, accurate profile.
>
> **Finally**, I score and segment — I build a lead scoring model based on your ICP so your reps know: these are your Hot leads, call them first. These are Warm, nurture them. These are Cold, archive them."

### The Close

> "The whole pilot takes about 4 weeks on a slice of your data. You get back clean data, a scored lead list, and a health report showing exactly what changed. If you see value, we scale it and I maintain it monthly. If not, you keep everything I built. No risk."

## 3. DISCOVERY QUESTIONS (ask these before pitching)

Use these to figure out their biggest pain and tailor your pitch:

1. **"When your reps open Salesforce, do they trust what they see?"**
   - If no → they need cleansing + enrichment
   - If yes → they might need scoring/segmentation or ongoing maintenance

2. **"What percentage of your outbound emails bounce?"**
   - >5% = their email data is bad, clear enrichment opportunity

3. **"How do your reps decide who to call first?"**
   - If "gut feel" or "whatever's on top" → they need lead scoring

4. **"Do you have duplicate accounts or contacts?"**
   - Almost always yes. Follow up: "Do you know how many?"

5. **"When was the last time someone cleaned up your CRM?"**
   - If "never" or "years ago" → full pilot opportunity

6. **"Are you using any AI tools, lead scoring, or intent data?"**
   - If yes but results are bad → "Those tools are only as good as the data feeding them"
   - If no → "Clean data is the foundation before any of that works"

7. **"What's your most expensive problem right now — missed leads, bad forecasting, or wasted rep time?"**
   - Their answer tells you which outcome to emphasize

## 4. OBJECTION HANDLING

### "We already have Clearbit / ZoomInfo / Clay"

> "That's great — those are solid enrichment tools. But here's what I see with most teams: you've got the tool, but nobody set up the rules for when to overwrite data, how to handle duplicates, or how to standardize what comes back. So you're paying for enrichment but your data still doesn't match. I don't replace those tools — I make them actually deliver by building the workflow, rules, and monitoring around them."

### "We have a RevOps person / team for that"

> "Most RevOps teams I work with are pulled in 10 directions — reporting, process, tool admin, forecasting. Data hygiene always falls to the bottom because it's unglamorous work. I take that specific piece off their plate so they can focus on strategy. I'm not replacing your RevOps person; I'm giving them back 10+ hours a week."

### "We don't have budget for this"

> "I totally get that. Let me ask — how many hours per week do your reps spend researching contacts before calling? If it's even 2 hours per rep, across your team that's [X] hours a month of selling time lost. At your average deal size, even one extra closed deal from better data pays for the entire engagement multiple times over. That's why I start with a pilot — you see the numbers before committing to anything bigger."

### "How is this different from hiring someone on Upwork?"

> "A freelancer can do data entry. What I do is build a repeatable system: automated cleaning scripts, enrichment workflows, scoring models, and monitoring dashboards. When I'm done, your data doesn't just get cleaned once — it stays clean. And I understand the industrial / B2B sales context, so I'm building scoring and segmentation that actually matches how your buyers behave."

### "Can you just give us the tools / scripts?"

> "Absolutely — everything I build is yours. But tools without someone running them is how most companies ended up with messy data in the first place. The real value is the ongoing ownership: I monitor data quality weekly, re-enrich quarterly, and adjust scoring as your market changes. That's the retainer piece, and it's what keeps the data from decaying again."

### "Our data isn't that bad"

> "That's possible — and the audit will tell us for sure. I've never walked into a Salesforce org with more than 10K records that didn't have at least 15% duplicates and 30% field gaps. But I'm happy to run a free assessment on a sample. If your data is clean, I'll tell you and we shake hands. If it's not, you'll see exactly where the leaks are."
