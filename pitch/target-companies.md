# Who To Target & What You Do For Each

*Your prospecting playbook — company by company*

---

## THE 8 COMPANY TYPES YOU TARGET

When you drive through industrial areas, you'll see these types of businesses.
Here's exactly what each one needs, what you pitch, and what you deliver.

---

## 1. MANUFACTURERS (Metal Fabrication, Plastics, Food, Electronics, etc.)

**What they look like:** Big buildings, loading docks, company trucks, names like "Precision Metals Inc" or "Pacific Steel Works." Often have 50-500+ employees.

**Their data problems:**
- Sales team uses Salesforce/CRM but it's disconnected from their ERP (production/inventory system)
- Reps promise delivery dates without knowing production capacity
- Quoting is manual — one steel company had to sort through 17,000 SKUs in Excel for every quote
- Forecast spreadsheets don't match CRM numbers
- Contacts for distributors, dealers, and direct customers are all mixed together with no segmentation

**What you pitch them:**
> "Your sales team is working off bad data. I can audit your CRM, show you exactly how many duplicates, missing contacts, and broken records you have, then clean it up and build a scoring system so your reps know which accounts to call first."

**What you actually do:**
- Audit their Salesforce leads/contacts/accounts
- Deduplicate distributor and dealer records
- Normalize product categories, territories, and account types
- Enrich missing buyer contacts (purchasing managers, plant managers, engineers)
- Build scoring model weighted toward purchasing authority and order history
- Deliver dashboard showing account health and territory coverage

**Your tier:** Standard Pilot ($7,500) or Enterprise ($15,000) — they have big databases
**Retainer potential:** High — they get new leads from trade shows and distributors constantly

**Who to talk to:** VP of Sales, Sales Operations Manager, Director of Business Development

---

## 2. WHOLESALE DISTRIBUTORS (Industrial Supply, Building Materials, Auto Parts)

**What they look like:** Large warehouses with truck bays, names like "ProFlow Supply" or "Westside Auto Parts." Move physical products to other businesses.

**Their data problems:**
- 62% of distributors using generic CRMs report "significant gaps in inventory and order visibility"
- Only 9% rate their analytics as "excellent"
- 55% can't identify at-risk customers
- Managing thousands of SKUs with complex tiered pricing across bill-to/ship-to accounts
- Reps track deals manually because CRM adds work without visibility
- Customer data scattered across ERP, CRM, spreadsheets, and rep notebooks

**What you pitch them:**
> "You have customers buying less and you don't know which ones are at risk until they're gone. I clean up your contact data, enrich it, and build a scoring system that flags accounts slipping away before you lose them."

**What you actually do:**
- Audit customer/account data across CRM and ERP exports
- Deduplicate ship-to/bill-to accounts that are the same company
- Normalize product categories and customer segments
- Enrich buyer contacts — find the actual purchasing decision-makers
- Build churn risk scoring: flag accounts whose order frequency or volume is declining
- Create a "Top 50 at-risk accounts" report their reps can act on immediately

**Your tier:** Standard Pilot ($7,500) — they typically have 10K-50K customer records
**Retainer potential:** Very high — they need monthly monitoring of account health

**Who to talk to:** Sales Manager, VP of Sales, General Manager, Owner

---

## 3. CONSTRUCTION COMPANIES & GENERAL CONTRACTORS

**What they look like:** Offices near industrial areas, company trucks with logos, names like "Vanguard Construction" or "Titan Concrete." Mix of office staff and field crews.

**Their data problems:**
- Subcontractor and vendor databases are a mess — same company listed 5 different ways
- Project data lives in spreadsheets, PM software, email, and someone's head
- No standardized process or terminology across teams
- Lead data from bid sites, referrals, and events dumped in CRM with no enrichment
- Can't easily see: which GCs give us work, which subs perform best, which markets are growing

**What you pitch them:**
> "Your subcontractor and vendor lists are full of duplicates, missing contacts, and outdated info. I clean it up so you know exactly who's in your network, who you should be bidding with, and where the gaps are."

**What you actually do:**
- Audit their vendor/sub database and CRM contacts
- Deduplicate company records (same sub listed with different names/addresses)
- Normalize trade categories (HVAC, Electrical, Plumbing, Concrete, etc.)
- Enrich missing contacts — find estimators, project managers, owners
- Build segmentation by trade, region, and past project type
- Deliver a clean "Bid Network" database they can use for future projects

**Your tier:** Basic Pilot ($3,500) for small GCs, Standard ($7,500) for large ones
**Retainer potential:** Medium — project-based business, but retainer for keeping vendor list fresh

**Who to talk to:** Owner, Estimating Manager, Business Development, Office Manager

## 4. STAFFING & RECRUITING AGENCIES

**What they look like:** Office spaces in or near industrial areas. Focus on placing temp workers, skilled trades, or professional staff. Names like "Apex Staffing" or "ProForce Personnel."

**Their data problems:**
- Up to 30% of candidate records lack key contact details
- 60%+ of recruiters say incomplete data blocks effective outreach
- Duplicate candidate records everywhere — same person applied 3 times over 2 years
- Missing standardized skills, pay ranges, availability, work authorization
- Teams with bad data are half as productive as those with enriched records
- Reps source externally because they can't find qualified candidates already in their database

**What you pitch them:**
> "You're paying to source new candidates when you've got thousands already in your database you can't find because the data is a mess. I clean and enrich your candidate records so your recruiters fill roles from what you already have."

**What you actually do:**
- Audit their ATS/CRM for completeness, duplicates, and stale records
- Deduplicate candidate records using name + email + phone matching
- Standardize skills, job titles, and trade categories
- Enrich missing contact info (current phone, email, LinkedIn)
- Flag stale candidates (haven't been contacted in 12+ months) for re-engagement
- Build segments: by trade/skill, availability, location, pay range

**Your tier:** Basic Pilot ($3,500) — most have 5K-20K candidate records
**Retainer potential:** Very high — new candidates enter daily, data decays fast

**Who to talk to:** Branch Manager, Recruiting Manager, Owner

---

## 5. LOGISTICS & FREIGHT COMPANIES

**What they look like:** Truck yards, warehouses, loading docks. Names like "Atlas Logistics" or "SwiftCargo Freight." Lots of trucks and trailers.

**Their data problems:**
- Customer and shipper contact data fragmented across dispatch software, CRM, email
- Driver/carrier databases are duplicated and outdated
- Can't easily segment customers by lane, volume, or profitability
- Sales reps don't know which lanes are profitable or which customers are at risk of leaving
- Broker/carrier relationships tracked informally

**What you pitch them:**
> "Your customer and carrier data is spread across 5 different systems. I consolidate, clean, and enrich it so your sales team knows which shippers to pursue and which lanes make you the most money."

**What you actually do:**
- Audit their customer, carrier, and broker databases
- Deduplicate across dispatch, TMS, and CRM systems
- Normalize lanes, regions, and service types
- Enrich shipper contacts — find logistics managers, supply chain directors
- Build customer value scoring based on volume, lane, and profitability
- Deliver a "Top 100 prospects" list for their sales team

**Your tier:** Standard Pilot ($7,500) — they typically have complex multi-system data
**Retainer potential:** High — carrier and customer data changes constantly

**Who to talk to:** VP of Sales, Operations Manager, Owner, Business Development

## 6. HVAC / PLUMBING / ELECTRICAL CONTRACTORS (Trade Companies)

**What they look like:** Shop + office combo. Company vans/trucks with branding. Names like "BlueLine Electrical" or "ProFlow Plumbing." 10-200 employees typically.

**Their data problems:**
- Customer records from 10+ years of service calls, never cleaned
- Same address listed multiple times (tenant changed but address didn't)
- No segmentation between commercial vs residential, new construction vs service
- Leads from Google, HomeAdvisor, referrals all dumped in with no enrichment
- Can't tell which neighborhoods, building types, or customer segments are most profitable
- Maintenance agreement customers mixed with one-time callers

**What you pitch them:**
> "You've got years of customer data in your system but you're not using it. I clean it up and show you which neighborhoods give you the most recurring revenue, which customers are due for maintenance, and which leads are most likely to buy."

**What you actually do:**
- Audit their customer database (often ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or a basic CRM)
- Deduplicate address and customer records
- Segment: commercial vs residential, service agreement vs one-time, new construction vs retrofit
- Enrich commercial contacts — find facility managers, property managers, building owners
- Build a "next best action" list: who's due for seasonal maintenance, who hasn't called in 2 years
- Map customer density by zip code to optimize service routes and marketing spend

**Your tier:** Basic Pilot ($3,500) — most have 5K-15K customer records
**Retainer potential:** Medium — seasonal, but valuable for maintenance agreement upselling

**Who to talk to:** Owner, Office Manager, Sales Manager

## 7. ENERGY / OIL & GAS / CHEMICAL COMPANIES

**What they look like:** Facilities with tanks, pipes, security gates. Names like "Summit Energy" or "Sterling Chemical." Usually 100+ employees with strict safety protocols.

**Their data problems:**
- MRO (maintenance, repair, operations) parts data is a nightmare — 12-15% overstocking from bad data
- Vendor/supplier databases have duplicates and inconsistent naming
- Equipment maintenance logs are unstructured, blocking predictive maintenance AI
- Safety inspection and compliance data scattered across systems
- Sales/customer data often managed at corporate level but messy at facility level

**What you pitch them:**
> "Bad parts data is costing you millions in overstocking and surprise breakdowns. I clean and standardize your MRO and vendor data so your maintenance AI works, your purchasing team stops ordering duplicates, and you pass audits without scrambling."

**What you actually do:**
- Audit MRO parts master data and vendor databases
- Deduplicate parts (same item listed under 3 different part numbers)
- Standardize part descriptions, categories, and units of measure
- Normalize vendor/supplier records
- Prepare data for predictive maintenance models (clean + structure maintenance logs)
- Deliver compliance-ready audit trail of all changes made

**Your tier:** Enterprise Pilot ($15,000) — complex, high-value, regulated
**Retainer potential:** Very high — regulatory requirements demand ongoing data governance

**Who to talk to:** Maintenance Manager, Purchasing Manager, Plant Manager, VP Operations

---

## 8. COMMERCIAL REAL ESTATE & PROPERTY MANAGEMENT

**What they look like:** Office buildings near industrial areas managing commercial properties. Names like "HarborView Properties" or tenant directories at business parks.

**Their data problems:**
- Tenant and prospect databases full of outdated contacts (people leave companies)
- Property listings and availabilities tracked in spreadsheets
- Broker contact databases duplicated and stale
- Can't segment prospects by industry, company size, or space needs
- Lead source tracking is inconsistent (CoStar, LoopNet, direct, referrals all mixed)

**What you pitch them:**
> "Your tenant and prospect database is full of people who left their companies two years ago. I clean it, find the current decision-makers, and score your prospects so your brokers chase the right deals."

**What you actually do:**
- Audit tenant, prospect, and broker contact databases
- Deduplicate and merge company records
- Enrich with current contacts — find real estate directors, facilities managers, CFOs
- Build prospect scoring based on company size, growth signals, and space needs
- Deliver a "Hot prospect" list for active listings

**Your tier:** Basic Pilot ($3,500) or Standard ($7,500)
**Retainer potential:** Medium — quarterly re-enrichment aligns with leasing cycles

**Who to talk to:** Brokerage Director, Leasing Manager, Owner

## HOW TO FIND THEM THIS WEEK

### Step 1: Drive the industrial areas (you already do this)
Write down every company name you see. Take photos of building signs. Note what kind of business it is.

### Step 2: Look them up
For each company:
- Google their name → website, size, what they do
- LinkedIn → find VP Sales, Sales Manager, Operations Manager, Owner
- Check if they use Salesforce (look at job postings mentioning "Salesforce" or check on BuiltWith/Wappalyzer)

### Step 3: Sort into tiers
- **Tier A (pitch first):** Companies with 50+ employees that clearly have a sales team
- **Tier B (pitch second):** Companies with 20-50 employees, owner-operated
- **Tier C (later):** Small shops with <20 people — probably don't have CRM yet

### Step 4: Reach out
- **Walk-ins** for small/mid companies — ask for the owner or sales manager
- **LinkedIn message** for larger companies — connect with VP Sales or RevOps
- **Cold email** using the template in `pitch-script.md`
- **Ask one question:** "When was the last time someone cleaned up your CRM?"

### Step 5: Offer the free assessment
- "I'll audit a sample of your data for free. Takes me a day. You get a health score and I show you exactly what's broken. No commitment."
- This costs you 3 hours. If even 1 in 5 converts to a pilot, that's $3,500-$7,500 for 15 hours of free work.
